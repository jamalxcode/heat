About this oil and gas heatmap

heat.sala.company/energy is a free heatmap of the main energy prices: crude oil, diesel, gasoline and natural gas, from the futures markets where they are set. Each tile combines the price move with technical indicators and suggested stop-losses.

Which prices does the energy heatmap show?

Six front-month futures: WTI crude oil (the US benchmark) and Brent crude oil (the world benchmark) in US dollars per barrel, New York Harbor ultra-low-sulphur diesel and RBOB gasoline in US dollars per gallon, US natural gas at Henry Hub in US dollars per MMBtu, and European natural gas at the Dutch TTF hub, where much of Europe's LNG is priced, in euros per MWh.

Why aren't Russian Urals, Dubai or Asian LNG prices shown?

Only prices that are at most about 30 minutes old are shown. Urals, Dubai, Oman, Murban, Canadian WCS, Asian LNG (JKM) and European diesel are published by paid price agencies; the free versions are days or months late, so they are left out rather than shown out of date.

Where do the prices come from, and how often are they updated?

From Yahoo Finance's public futures data, every 10 minutes while the markets are open (Sunday evening to Friday afternoon, US time). The exchanges delay these quotes by about 10 minutes, so a price here is up to about 20 to 30 minutes old. Nothing here is real-time.

What do drivers pay at the pump?

Below the futures, a weekly table gives petrol and diesel pump prices, taxes included, for the United States, the United Kingdom and all 27 EU countries, converted to US dollars per litre (with the local price beside it): from the EU Weekly Oil Bulletin, the UK government and the US Energy Information Administration. A second table has US wholesale spot prices for diesel, jet fuel, gasoline, heating oil and propane, official but about a week behind. Gulf states set their pump prices by monthly announcement rather than publishing data, so they are not included yet.

What does front month mean?

The futures contract closest to expiry, which is the price the market usually quotes. Each month it rolls into the next contract, so a chart can show a small step on the roll day, bigger for natural gas.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an experimental, work-in-progress tool. Prices can be late, wrong or missing.
